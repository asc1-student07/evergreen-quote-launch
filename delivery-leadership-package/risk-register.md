# Risk Register: Evergreen Insurance Instant Quote Launch

| # | Risk | Owner | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation | Trigger to escalate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Launch depends on a running database.** The client now reads and saves quotes through the API and MongoDB. If either is down, the recent-quotes panel is empty and the product looks broken even though nothing is wrong with the front end. | Delivery Lead | M | H | Health check before every demo or stakeholder session: API responds, seeded quotes return. Document the start-up order (database, API, client) and who owns each. | Quotes fail to load and the cause isn't found in 15 minutes → bring in the API/platform owner. |
| R2 | **Client and API disagree on the data contract.** The integration already surfaced one mismatch (the API sends `_id`, the client expected `id`). It was caught only because the type-check gate exists. Others may be hiding in fields the happy path doesn't exercise. | Engineering Lead | M | H | Type-check (client) and API tests run after every integration change and on every push in CI. | Either check red at end of day → no push; name it at the next check-in. |
| R3 | **Rates are kept in multiple places.** The same rate table exists separately in the client and in the API, and each has to be edited by hand. They were aligned to 90 / 135 / 70 per the sponsor's instruction, but nothing enforces that they stay aligned. Any future rate change risks the two tables diverging, so customers could see different prices for the same inputs. This risk remains until a real rating engine becomes the single source of rates. | Engineering Lead + Product | M | H | Treat the API's rates as the source of truth. Make any rate change in both places in the same change, and record the values in the commit message. Spot-check auto / age 35 / $50,000 = $450.00 in both the estimate and the saved quote after every rate change. | Any input where the estimate and the saved premium differ → freeze rate changes until resolved and notify the sponsor. Any request for a new coverage type or rate → raise the rating engine as a prerequisite before adding to the duplication. |
| R4 | **Historical quotes carry premiums from an older rate table.** Seeded quotes were priced under previous rates. A customer or reviewer comparing an old saved quote to a fresh one for the same inputs will see different numbers. | Product Owner | H | M | Make clear in the product and in launch notes that saved premiums are point-in-time. Confirm with the sponsor whether old quotes should be labeled, migrated or left alone. | A stakeholder or customer questions a saved premium → treat it as a pricing defect until proven otherwise. |
| R6 | **API has no access control or abuse protection.** It accepts quote creation from any caller, and the database stores whatever passes validation. | Security / Platform Lead | M | H | Confirm with the sponsor that exposure is internal only for now. Raise authentication, rate limiting and validation hardening as launch-gate items. | Any plan to expose the API outside the internal network without these controls → no-go. |
| R7 | **The cluster's database and the VM's database are different databases behind the same product.** Same image, different environment, different data. The cluster starts empty and holds only what is saved into it. A demo that moves between environments, or a stakeholder comparing them, will see different quotes and may read it as a defect. | Delivery Lead | H | M | Say plainly in the demo plan which environment each screen is showing and what data it holds. Decide in advance whether the cluster demo starts empty or is seeded, and rehearse that path. Never imply the environments share data. | A stakeholder asks why the quotes differ between two views → treat it as a gap in the demo plan, not an engineering fault. |

## How this register is used

Reviewed at each daily check-in and after any change in scope or priorities. Risks that fire move to the top of the next status update with the trigger named. The register lives in the repo so stakeholders can see what the team was watching, not just what went wrong.
